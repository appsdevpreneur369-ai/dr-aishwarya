An implant replaces the root of a missing tooth with a small titanium post. Once the bone has bonded to it, a crown is fixed on top. The process takes a few months, mostly because bone heals at its own pace, but you only spend a handful of short visits in the chair.

## 1. Consultation and planning

Dr. Aishwarya examines the gap, the neighbouring teeth and the gums. An X-ray, and often a 3D scan, shows how much bone is available and where nerves and sinuses lie. You will hear whether an implant suits you, which alternatives exist, how many visits to expect and the cost.

Healthy gums come first. If there is gum disease elsewhere in the mouth, it is treated before the implant is placed.

## 2. Preparing the site (only if needed)

If the bone has thinned since the tooth was lost, a **bone graft** may be placed to build it up. This usually needs a few months to heal before the implant goes in.

## 3. Placing the implant

Under local anaesthetic, a small opening is made in the gum and the implant is placed in the bone. Most people are surprised how quick this is. Afterwards you can expect some soreness and mild swelling for a few days, eased by cold packs and the medicine you are prescribed.

## 4. Healing time

Over the next few months the bone grows tightly around the implant. You can usually wear a temporary tooth during this time so there is no visible gap.

## 5. The final tooth

Once healed, a connector (abutment) is attached and impressions or a scan are taken. Your crown, bridge or denture is then made to match your other teeth and fixed in place.

## Looking after it

An implant cannot decay, but the gum around it can become inflamed just like gums around natural teeth. Brushing, cleaning between teeth and regular check-ups are what keep an implant healthy for many years.
