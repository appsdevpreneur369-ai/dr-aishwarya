"Laser" sounds high-tech, and in dentistry it is a genuinely useful tool. But it is a tool, not a cure-all. Here is a plain guide to where lasers help and where conventional treatment is still better.

## How a dental laser works

A dental laser produces a narrow beam of light energy. Aimed at soft tissue such as the gum, it can cut, reshape or remove tissue precisely. As it works it seals small blood vessels and nerve endings, which is why laser procedures often bleed less.

## Where lasers help

- **Reshaping the gums** for a more even smile, or to show more of a short tooth
- **Gum pocket treatment**, alongside deep cleaning, to reduce bacteria in infected pockets
- **Frenectomy**: releasing a tight tongue-tie or lip-tie
- **Painful ulcers and cold sores**, where laser treatment may ease discomfort
- **Uncovering an implant** before the final tooth is fitted

## What it feels like

Many patients find laser procedures more comfortable than a scalpel, and there may be fewer stitches. Most treatments still use local anaesthetic, and everyone in the room wears protective glasses.

## What lasers don't replace

A laser does not replace brushing, cleaning between the teeth or regular check-ups. For many treatments, such as most fillings, crowns or extractions, conventional methods remain the standard. A trained dentist will recommend the method that suits your problem, not the most impressive-sounding one.
